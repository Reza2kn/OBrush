# C255 held-stroke pixel mismatch: static diagnosis

The sixth C255 browser receipt passed a bounded contact gate but has a large appearance mismatch. The independently imported active triangle and normalized +0.5 mirror plane were numerically aligned; the reference changed 34 RGB pixels in the preregistered crop, while OBrush changed 2,340 (`68.8235×`). This count is **not** a brush-quality score: it is a same-host before/after crop count, and the two renderers, camera scale, lighting and silhouette differ. The full symmetry-offset and line ledger rows remain open.

The first cause is observable before a stroke. In the same import, OBrush Solo + Frame left its Source camera eye at Z `11108.3411`; the 0.29-local-unit frozen stroke occupied only `1.3642` CSS pixels. SculptGL projected it to `51.8683` pixels. Source's pinned `SculptBase.sculptStroke()` gate requires movement greater than `0.15 × 250 = 37.5` pixels, so OBrush logged zero dabs at that framing. Fifteen ordinary wheel-up events in OBrush moved its eye to Z `261.7314` and projected the path to `58.0068` pixels, allowing exactly one Source dab. This is a concrete framing disparity even though OBrush's view-only plane position matches.

After that zoom, the image projections are close enough to isolate another mismatch. Dividing each recorded stroke span by its authored local span `0.29` gives reference `178.8562` and OBrush `200.0235` CSS pixels per local unit, an `11.83%` difference. But the reference mirrored picker reports local radius squared `0.12211026693735978` (radius `0.34944279494`), while OBrush's completed pass reports `1.5621323064788237` (radius `1.24985291394`). In visible screen units these are approximately `62.5000` and `250.0000` CSS pixels respectively, almost exactly `4×`. The mismatch in projected brush footprint is therefore much larger than the residual camera scale difference. OBrush's larger local footprint is a plausible major contributor to its larger changed-pixel region. The present receipt does not include exact OBrush vertex displacement, so it does not prove how much of the `68.82×` area ratio that footprint explains.

Both implementations nominally display radius `250`, intensity `0.5`, X symmetry on, ordinary clay Draw, accumulate on, alpha None and mouse pressure. SculptGL's pinned `SculptBase.getScreenRadius()` returns `_radius × pixelRatio`, `Picking.computeWorldRadius2()` projects and unprojects an X offset at the hit depth, then divides by mesh scale squared; `SculptBase.makeStroke()` copies the primary radius to the mirror picker. OBrush's shared core similarly projects and unprojects the captured screen radius, with a mouse-pressure factor of 0.75 yielding no change at pressure 0.5. The factor-of-four difference is **observed**, but the current receipt does not show the intermediate reference camera/projected-radius values needed to locate its exact origin. The reference's scalar `_pixelRatio`, canvas backing dimensions, camera width/height, projected hit depth, `getScreenRadius()`, `getScale2()`, and primary/mirror radius at `makeStroke()` are the discriminating measurements. Do not apply a guessed quarter-scale correction to OBrush.

A bounded next paired gate should keep this independent SGL fixture, +0.5 offset, mask workflow, isolated active face, 250 nominal radius and frozen stroke path. Instrument read-only reference `project(unproject(...))` and the exact `Picking.computeWorldRadius2()` inputs at each dab; record OBrush's corresponding view, pressure and local radius. Add active three-vertex arrays before/after on both sides (via normal save/export or GPU readback, without mutating document internals), and capture screenshots with cursor/guide state fixed. First require radius-chain and dab-count explanations, then preregister a correction and a fresh pixel/vertex pair. Renderer coloration and crop location remain separate until the geometry deltas agree.
